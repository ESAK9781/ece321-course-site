2. Course Objectives & Prerequisites Foundations

Course Objectives

Upon successful completion of ECE 321, cadets will be able to:

  • Design experiments (laboratories) to collect, analyze, and interpret data, in order to effectively report on correct operation of a specified circuit or verification of a given theory;
  • Conduct experiments using laboratory equipment to obtain appropriate data for use in characterizing, analyzing, and troubleshooting circuits using discrete active electronic devices in order to effectively report on correct operation of a specified circuit or verification of a given theory;
  • Design analog and digital circuits to meet various requirements by applying appropriate diode and field effect transistor models;
  • Demonstrate the ability to identify and analyze situations where ethical and professional standards of conduct must guide the behavior of officers in the Air Force engineering environment;
  • Use computer-based analysis tools to verify and optimize the characteristics of electronic devices and circuits.

Prerequisite Courses

  • ECE 245: Differential Equations with Circuit Applications I (Circuits 1)
  • ECE 281: Digital Design and Computer Architecture
  • Physics 215: General Physics II
  • Chemistry 100: General Chemistry I

Prerequisites by Topic

  • Circuits: Voltage/Current/Power; Ohm’s Law, KVL/KCL; Circuit analysis techniques: voltage/current dividers, nodal and mesh analysis, Thevenin analysis, superposition, source transformation; dependent sources; Capacitance.
  • Digital: Logic gates, truth tables, sum of products, product of sums, Boolean algebra; SR Latch; flip flops; propagation delay; Decoders.
  • Physics: Valence and conduction bands; Charge Carriers; Electric fields; Resistivity; Current Density.
  • Chemistry: Atomic structure; Crystal structure; Periodic Table; Chemical bonding.

5. Attendance, EI, and Documentation Policies

Extra Instruction (EI)

It is best to schedule a time after class, via Teams, or by phone, though walk-ins are always welcome. Please try to maximize your visit time by having specific questions. It is advised to review the text, notes, and homework prior to the meeting.

Attendance & Absences

Inform your instructor of absences in advance via email/Teams. Include the reason for absence and any relevant SCA. Check the SCA to see if instructor permission is required; if so, make the request prior to the absence. Failure to obtain permission results in an Unexcused Absence in CAS.

Due dates are listed on the syllabus. If missing class, submit work beforehand. You are responsible for obtaining notes and checking for new assignments.

Collaboration & Documentation

You may receive help from any DFEC faculty member or any student currently enrolled in ECE 321.

Mandatory Documentation: Document all help received on all work submitted for grading in accordance with Dean of Faculty (DF) policies, or write “none” if your work was completely an individual effort.

6. Course Components, Exams & Labs

Homework & Quizzes

Homework: Turn in assignments at the start of class, front right desk. Late penalties commence from the point where the instructor collects assignments. There are 13 homework sets, each typically containing textbook problems plus conceptual questions.

Quizzes: Graded quizzes will be given periodically throughout the semester as noted on the syllabus. You are expected to have an appropriate calculator for each quiz. The instructor will announce whether an equation sheet is provided and which material is assessed.

Graded Reviews & Final Exam

Graded Reviews (GR1 & GR2): Both exams are closed book; however, official equation sheets will be provided in advance for use during the exam. Operational, approved calculators are required.

Final Exam & Lab Practical: Comprehensive final exam administered during the designated finals week. Equation sheets are provided. A 1-hour lab practical during the final week of classes will count towards your final exam score (cadets will build a circuit, instrument it, collect data, and draw conclusions).

Laboratories & Practical Exercises

Formal Labs (4 Exercises): Covering diodes, transistors, multi-stage amplifiers, and digital circuits. Performed in groups of 2 with one formal report handed in per group. Mr. Jacobs will support lab sessions.

Additional Lab Exercises (PEs): Practical activities to gain experience in building circuits, reading component datasheets, instrumenting, and taking bench measurements. These factor into your Progress and Final Lab score.

Special Topics & Ethics

Special Topic 5-Min Presentation: A list of 13 electronics-related topics will be circulated early in the semester. Each cadet selects a topic and prepares a talk not exceeding 5 minutes. Evaluated on technical accuracy, visual/oral delivery, and professionalism (typically 5–7 slides with references).

Ethics Assignments: Discussion of ethical and professional responsibilities as both an engineer and an Air Force officer. Two case studies are assigned on the syllabus and count as homework grades.

7. Classroom Etiquette

  • Computers: Computers should normally be in tablet mode unless the instructor specifically requests you to perform an internet search, run simulation software, or if you are actively taking notes.
  • Food & Drink: Food or drink is NEVER allowed at the lab stations under any circumstances. Dean-approved drinks in closed containers are allowed at classroom lecture desks; food is generally NOT allowed in the classroom.
  • Professionalism: No sleeping — please treat classroom and laboratory time with the professional demeanor expected of future Air Force officers.
  • Disruptions: Keep side conversations to a minimum to avoid distracting your fellow cadets or the instructor.
  • Engagement: Questions are always welcomed and encouraged! Actively engage with in-class practice problems and collaborate with peers when instructed.
