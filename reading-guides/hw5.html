Problem 5.47 Lesson 10 · p. 299 · §5.3

DC design at the saturation boundary in the fixed-gate NMOS circuit.

Problem statement

D5.47 It is required to operate the transistor in the circuit of Fig. P5.46 at the edge of saturation with ID=50\mu A. If Vt=0.4V, find the required value of RD.

Similar to 5.47 same task, new values

Similar problem (new numbers)

In the Figure P5.46 circuit, tie the gate and drain resistor supply to +1.5 V and ground the source. For V_t = 0.5 V and I_D = 40 μA, choose R_D so the transistor is at the edge of saturation.

1 · Gate overdrive and boundary voltage

V_GS=1.5 V, so V_OV=1.5−0.5=1.0 V. At the edge of saturation, V_DS=V_OV=1.0 V.

2 · Apply the drain-loop voltage relation

With the source at ground, the drain voltage is 1.0 V. The resistor drop is 1.5−1.0=0.5 V. Thus R_D=0.5 V/40 μA=12.5 kΩ.

Reading · 5.47

Summarized · pp. 262–264, 273–275, 277–278

For DC MOSFET circuits, the chapter recommends first identifying the likely region and then checking it against the terminal voltages. In saturation the ideal current follows the square-law relation. The boundary occurs when drain-to-source voltage equals overdrive. Resistor voltages follow from KVL and Ohm’s law; the source at ground makes the drain-loop relation direct.

Expansion note: What does “edge” specify?

It names the equality between the triode and saturation boundary variables. For a fixed gate bias, it pins down the drain voltage at transition.
