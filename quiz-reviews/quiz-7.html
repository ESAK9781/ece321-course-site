Noise margins (Lesson 30), propagation delay (Lesson 31) and transistor sizing (Lesson 32)

Quiz 7 (Lesson 33) covers Lessons 30 to 32: noise margins of matched and unmatched CMOS inverters, inverter propagation delay by the average-current and equivalent-resistance methods, and sizing the transistors of a logic gate from an optimized inverter. Each topic climbs the same ladder: the big idea in plain words, the facts and method to memorize, then questions from warm-up to quiz-style. Cover the steps and try each question before you read on. Hand calculations use the long-channel square-law model with λ=0. Values marked (chosen) are not in the notetakers.

  2. Propagation delay (Lesson 31)
  3. Sizing transistors in logic gates (Lesson 32)

1. Noise Margins and the CMOS VTC

Big idea. A logic gate must still read the right value when noise nudges its input. The voltage transfer characteristic (VTC) is flat near both rails and drops steeply in the middle. VIL and VIH are the two inputs where the slope is −1; the noise margins say how far a good output level sits from them.

Know these.

  • CMOS levels: VOH=VDD and VOL=0. Noise margins: NML=VIL−VOL and NMH=VOH−VIH.
  • Matched inverter (Kn=Kp, Vtn=|Vtp|=Vt): VIH=18(5VDD−2Vt), VIL=18(3VDD+2Vt), VM=VDD/2.
  • Unmatched: r=Kp/Kn and VM=Vtn+r(VDD−|Vtp|)1+r. A weaker PMOS (r<1) moves VM down, a stronger one moves it up.
  • To match, (W/L)p=(μn/μp)(W/L)n, usually 2 to 4 times (W/L)n.

Method.

  1. Matched inverter: put VDD and Vt into the two formulas, then subtract from the rails.
  2. Unmatched inverter: find r, then VM, then the shift ΔVM=VM−VDD/2.
  3. Slide the matched noise margins: NML≈NMmatched+ΔVM and NMH≈NMmatched−ΔVM.
  4. Sanity check: VOL<VIL<VM<VIH<VOH and both margins positive.

Question 1.1 (warm-up). What are the noise margins of an inverter with these measured levels?

Given
VOL=0.10, VIL=0.40, VIH=0.55, VOH=0.95V (chosen).
Find
NML, NMH and the worst case.
  1. Step 1

    Pair each output level with its input threshold.

    NML=VIL−VOL=0.40−0.10=0.30V,NMH=VOH−VIH=0.95−0.55=0.40V

    A low output goes with VIL, a high output with VIH.

  2. Step 2

    Take the smaller.

    NMworst=min(0.30,0.40)=0.30V

    Both are positive, so the gate can drive an identical gate.

Question 1.2 (standard). Find VIL, VIH and both noise margins of a matched CMOS inverter.

Given
VDD=1.0V, Vtn=|Vtp|=Vt=0.35V, Kn=Kp (the 65-nm values of Lesson 30).
Find
VM, VIH, VIL, NMH, NML.
  1. Step 1

    Switching point.

    VM=VDD2=0.5V

    Matched devices make the curve symmetric about the middle.

  2. Step 2

    Input thresholds.

    VIH=18(5VDD−2Vt)=18(5−0.70)=0.5375V,VIL=18(3VDD+2Vt)=18(3+0.70)=0.4625V

    They are mirror images: VIL=VDD−VIH.

  3. Step 3

    Noise margins.

    NMH=1.0−0.5375=0.4625V,NML=0.4625−0=0.4625V

    About 93% of the ideal 0.5V. Order check: 0<0.4625<0.5<0.5375<1.0 ✓.

Question 1.3 (quiz-style). How wide must the PMOS be to match the NMOS, and what do you give up with Wp=Wn?

Given
65-nm process: VDD=1.0V, Vtn=|Vtp|=0.35V, μn=5.4μp, (W/L)n=1.5, L=65nm.
Find
(a) Wp to match; (b) area; (c) VM and NML for Wp=Wn; (d) area saved.
  1. Step 1

    (a) Size for Kn=Kp.

    Wn=1.5(65)=97.5nm,Wp=μnμpWn=5.4(97.5)=526.5nm

    Equal lengths mean the width ratio must equal the mobility ratio.

  2. Step 2

    (b) Area.

    A=L(Wn+Wp)=65(97.5+526.5)=40,560nm^2
  3. Step 3

    (c) Strength ratio and switching point for Wp=Wn.

    r=KpKn=15.4=0.430,VM=0.35+0.430(0.65)1.430=0.44V

    A PMOS five times weaker moves VM only 0.06V.

  4. Step 4

    Shift the margins.

    ΔVM=0.44−0.50=−0.06V,NML≈0.46−0.06=0.40V,NMH≈0.52V

    Exact solution: 0.39 and 0.54V, so the estimate is good.

  5. Step 5

    (d) Area saved.

    A=65(97.5+97.5)=12,675nm^2,1−12,67540,560=69%

    A small loss of NML buys a 69% smaller inverter.

Question 1.4 (quiz-style). Estimate the noise margins when the PMOS is only twice as wide as the NMOS.

Given
VDD=1.0V, Vt=0.35V, μn=3μp, (W/L)p=2(W/L)n (chosen).
Find
r, VM, NML, NMH.
  1. Step 1

    Strength ratio.

    KpKn=μp(W/L)pμn(W/L)n=23,r=0.816
  2. Step 2

    Switching point.

    VM=0.35+0.816(0.65)1.816=0.485V,ΔVM=−0.015V
  3. Step 3

    Slide the matched margins (0.4625V).

    NML≈0.4625−0.015=0.447V,NMH≈0.4625+0.015=0.478V

    A numerical solution gives 0.444 and 0.482V. Using 2× instead of 3× costs almost nothing in margin and saves area.

Check yourself

  1. What are VOH and VOL of a CMOS inverter?
    Answer

    VDD and 0.

  2. A PMOS wider than the matched width moves VM which way?
    Answer

    Up (a stronger PMOS pulls the output high longer), so NMH shrinks and NML grows.

  3. Which levels pair up in NML?
    Answer

    VIL−VOL.

2. Propagation Delay

Big idea. A gate output is a capacitor that the on-transistor must charge or discharge, and that takes time. The delay is measured between the 50% points of the input and output edges. The two directions differ: a falling output is tPHL (NMOS pulls down) and a rising output is tPLH (PMOS pulls up).

Know these.

  • 50% points give delays; 10% to 90% gives transition (rise or fall) times. tP=12(tPHL+tPLH).
  • Method 1, average current: tPHL=anCKnVDD, tPLH=apCKpVDD, a=274−3VtVDD+(VtVDD)2 (use |Vtp| for ap).
  • Method 2, equivalent resistance: tPHL=0.693RNC, tPLH=0.693RPC, with R=Reff(Weff/Leff)/(W/L).
  • Load: CINT=2Cgd2+2Cgd1+Cdb2+Cdb1 (Miller factor 2) and CEXT=(Cwire+Cg4+Cg3)×Fan\_Out; C=CINT+CEXT.

Method.

  1. Decide the direction: falling output is NMOS and tPHL, rising output is PMOS and tPLH.
  2. Find the load C (add the pieces if given).
  3. Method 1 needs K and a; Method 2 needs R scaled by W/L. Both give a delay proportional to C.
  4. Average for tP. Changing VDD requires Method 1: the Reff values were measured at one supply.

Question 2.1 (warm-up). Read tPHL, tPLH, tP and tTHL from these waveform readouts.

Given
Input rises through 50% at 20.0 and falls through 50% at 120.0ps. Output falls through 90%, 50%, 10% at 22.7, 38.0, 79.9ps and rises through 50% at 155.0ps (chosen).
Find
tPHL, tPLH, tP, tTHL.
  1. Step 1

    Delays: output 50% time minus input 50% time.

    tPHL=38.0−20.0=18.0ps,tPLH=155.0−120.0=35.0ps

    The output is slower rising, so the pull-up is the weaker device.

  2. Step 2

    Average.

    tP=12(18.0+35.0)=26.5ps
  3. Step 3

    Transition time (an edge duration, not a delay).

    tTHL=t10%−t90%=79.9−22.7=57.2ps

Question 2.2 (standard). Find the delays by both methods, then lower the supply to 0.8V (Example 17-2).

Given
VDD=1.0V, Vtn=|Vtp|=0.35V, kn′=5.4kp′=540µA/V², (W/L)n=1.5, (W/L)p=3, C=5fF; reference devices (W/L=1.5): Reff,N=8kΩ, Reff,P=24kΩ.
Find
tPHL, tPLH, tP by each method, then tP at 0.8V.
  1. Step 1

    Method 1: K and a.

    Kn=540(1.5)=810,Kp=100(3)=300µA/V²,a=21.75−3(0.35)+0.352=2.43

    kp′=540/5.4=100. The same a serves both devices because |Vtp|=Vtn.

  2. Step 2

    Method 1: delays.

    tPHL=2.43(5fF)(810µA/V²)(1)=15.0ps,tPLH=2.43(5)300=40.5ps,tP=27.8ps
  3. Step 3

    Method 2: scale the resistances.

    RN=8kΩ(1.5)1.5=8kΩ,RP=24kΩ(1.5)3=12kΩ

    Doubling the width halves the resistance.

  4. Step 4

    Method 2: delays.

    tPHL=0.693(8kΩ)(5fF)=27.7ps,tPLH=0.693(12)(5)=41.6ps,tP=34.7ps

    The tPLH values agree; Method 2 is longer for tPHL because its resistances come from the real technology.

  5. Step 5

    Lower VDD to 0.8V (Method 1 only).

    a=21.75−3(0.4375)+0.43752=3.18,tPHL=3.18(5)810(0.8)=24.5ps,tPLH=3.18(5)300(0.8)=66.3ps

    tP=45.4ps, about 63% slower: less power costs speed.

Question 2.3 (quiz-style). Find the total load capacitance of an inverter with fan-out 2 and the resulting tPHL.

Given
Driver: Cgd1=0.2, Cgd2=0.3, Cdb1=0.5, Cdb2=0.7fF. Each driven inverter: Cg3=0.9, Cg4=1.5fF. Cwire=0.8fF per branch, RN=8kΩ (all chosen).
Find
CINT, CEXT, CTOT, tPHL.
  1. Step 1

    Internal capacitance.

    CINT=2(0.3)+2(0.2)+0.7+0.5=2.2fF

    Gate-drain terms count twice (Miller effect).

  2. Step 2

    External capacitance.

    CEXT=(0.8+1.5+0.9)(2)=6.4fF
  3. Step 3

    Total and delay.

    CTOT=8.6fF,tPHL=0.693(8kΩ)(8.6fF)=47.7ps

    Compared with 27.7ps at 5fF: delay scales directly with C, so more fan-out is slower.

Check yourself

  1. Which method can you use when VDD changes?
    Answer

    Method 1, because VDD appears in its formula; the Reff of Method 2 were measured at one supply.

  2. Which delay does the PMOS set?
    Answer

    tPLH (rising output).

  3. A transition time of 57 ps and a delay of 18 ps: which is the edge duration?
    Answer

    The 57 ps (10% to 90%); the 18 ps is the 50%-to-50% delay.

3. Sizing Transistors in Logic Gates

Big idea. In a bigger gate some transistors sit in series, and series resistances add, so the gate would be slower than the optimized inverter. Widening a transistor lowers its resistance (R∝1/W), so we widen the devices until the worst-case path matches one inverter transistor.

Know these.

  • The inverter has (W/L)n=n and (W/L)p=p. Use the minimum L, so W=(W/L)L.
  • A path of k series devices needs each device k times wider: kp in the PUN, kn in the PDN.
  • A device in parallel with a series string must match the string’s resistance. A device on every path is sized by the longest path.
  • NAND needs less area than NOR, because its long stack is NMOS (ratio 4n) and p>n.

Method.

  1. Find the longest series path in each network and count its devices, k.
  2. Size every device on that path to k× the inverter ratio.
  3. Size any lone parallel device so its path totals one inverter resistance.
  4. Check every other path (each must be no worse than the inverter), then convert with W=(W/L)L.

Question 3.1 (warm-up). Size a four-input NOR gate (Lesson 32, Example 1).

Given
Inverter n=2, p=4, L=65nm.
Find
Ratio and width of each transistor.
  1. Step 1

    Worst-case paths: four PMOS in series in the PUN, one NMOS at a time in the PDN.

    kPUN=4,kPDN=1

    The PUN conducts only when all inputs are low. In the PDN the worst case is a single input high.

  2. Step 2

    Size.

    Wp=4pL=16(65)=1040nm,Wn=nL=2(65)=130nm

Question 3.2 (warm-up). Size a four-input NAND gate and compare it with the NOR (Example 2).

Given
The same inverter and L.
Find
Ratio and width of each transistor; total width against the NOR.
  1. Step 1

    Mirror image of the NOR.

    Wn=4nL=8(65)=520nm,Wp=pL=4(65)=260nm
  2. Step 2

    Compare total width.

    WNOR=4(1040)+4(130)=4680nm,WNAND=4(520)+4(260)=3120nm

    The NAND is about one third smaller because the long stack is NMOS, the narrower type.

Question 3.3 (standard). Size a complex gate and find its logic function (Example 3).

Given
PUN: A in series with (B in parallel with the series pair D, C). PDN: A in parallel with (B in series with the parallel pair C, D). Inverter n=2, p=4, L=65nm.
Find
Every device size and Y.
  1. Step 1

    PUN: longest path is A-D-C, so k=3.

    A,C,D:3p=12,W=12(65)=780nm

    A is on every path, so it takes this size too.

  2. Step 2

    B must make the A-B path equal Rinv.

    RB=Rinv−Rinv3=23Rinv⇒(W/L)B=1.5p=6,W=390nm

    Half the width of C and D, because the D-C branch has twice one device’s resistance.

  3. Step 3

    PDN: longest paths B-C and B-D, so k=2.

    B,C,D:2n=4,W=260nm;A:n=2,W=130nm

    A is a one-device path and keeps the inverter size.

  4. Step 4

    Function.

    Y=A+B(C+D)‾

    The PDN conducts when A, or B with (C or D), is high.

Question 3.4 (quiz-style). Size a five-input gate whose pull-up has a parallel branch inside a series path (Example 4).

Given
PUN: (A in parallel with the series string B, C, D) in series with E; PDN is the dual. Inverter n=2, p=4, L=65nm.
Find
Every device size.
  1. Step 1

    Longest PUN path is B-C-D-E, so k=4.

    B,C,D,E:4p=16,W=1040nm

    E is on every path, so it matches the longest one.

  2. Step 2

    A must equal the B-C-D string, which is 3× one device.

    WA=10403=347nm,(W/L)A=163=5.3

    Check the A-E path: 34Rinv+14Rinv=Rinv ✓.

  3. Step 3

    Dual PDN: A in series with (B∥C∥D), all in parallel with E. Longest path has 2 devices.

    A,B,C,D:2n=4,W=260nm;E:n=2,W=130nm
  4. Step 4

    Function.

    Y=A(B+C+D)+E‾

Check yourself

  1. In a 3-input NAND, how wide is each NMOS (inverter ratio n)?
    Answer

    3n (three in series); each PMOS stays p.

  2. Why is NAND preferred to NOR in CMOS?
    Answer

    Its long series stack is NMOS, which needs less width than the PMOS stack a NOR needs (p>n).

  3. A PUN path has 2 devices of ratio 2p each. Is it as good as the inverter?
    Answer

    Yes: two in series, each half the resistance of the inverter PMOS, total Rinv.

Quiz-Day Checklist

  • Noise margins. VOH=VDD, VOL=0. Matched: VIH=18(5VDD−2Vt), VIL=18(3VDD+2Vt). Unmatched: r, then VM, then shift the margins by ±ΔVM.
  • Delay. 50% points; tPHL is NMOS, tPLH is PMOS; Method 1 aC/(KVDD), Method 2 0.693RC; load C=CINT+CEXT; tP is the average; supply changes need Method 1.
  • Sizing. k series devices means k× the inverter ratio; a lone parallel device matches its string; verify every path; W=(W/L)L.
  • Show the general equation first, then the numbers, and check units (fF with kΩ gives ps).
