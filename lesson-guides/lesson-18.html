Lesson 18: Common-Source Amplifier Design, Part 2

18-1

Common Source Amplifier Design : Part 2

(*) Last time we found that without RS, the DC† operating point varied too much with Vt & Kn†

Expansion note: why the operating point moves when Vt and Kn change

Vt and Kn differ from one transistor to the next. With the gate set only by a divider, VGS is fixed, so ID=12Kn(VGS−Vt)2 changes directly with both. A source resistor RS fights the change: if ID rises, IDRS rises, VGS falls and the current is pulled back. That is the negative feedback the next lines refer to.

Proposal #1 : Add RS into the DC biasing circuit, then remove it from the AC small-signal circuit by shorting it with a bypass capacitor

|Avo| = gmRD

Expansion note: what bypassing RS does

For DC, a capacitor is an open circuit, so RS stays in the bias path and stabilizes ID. For the signal, a large capacitor looks like a short, so the source is effectively at ac ground and RS takes no part in the gain. The open-circuit gain is then the full gmRD. The notes work with the magnitude |Avo|; the stage itself inverts the signal.

Q. How do we choose the DC biasing voltages ?

18-2

(*) For saturation recall

VDS ≥ VOV

VGS − Vt = VOV

VGS = VOV + Vt

Expansion note: why VDS≥VOV keeps the transistor in saturation

The channel pinches off at the drain end when the gate-to-drain voltage falls to Vt, that is VGD=VGS−VDS≤Vt. Rearranged, VDS≥VGS−Vt=VOV. Below that the device is in the triode region and no longer acts as a good current source.

so to maximize swing† and give a good† voltage across RS to ensure our negative† Feedback loop is strong† enough, we will allocate VDD according to

VRD = VDS = VRS

Expansion note: why the supply is split into equal thirds

VRD, VDS and VRS add up to VDD. VDS sets how far the drain can swing before the device leaves saturation, VRD sets the gain through RD, and VRS sets how strong the feedback is. Giving each a third, VDD/3, balances the three.

Proposal #2 : CS-Amplifier Design with Partial RS Bypass

where we derived

Avo = −gmRD1+gmRS1

gm = Kn VOV

Expansion note: what RS1 and RS2 do

RS is split in two. RS1 is the part the signal still sees, because the capacitor bypasses only RS2. For DC the capacitor is open and the full RS=RS1+RS2 sets the bias, so the feedback that stabilizes ID is as strong as before. For the signal, the unbypassed RS1 divides the gain by 1+gmRS1.

Rule of Thumb (RoT) #1 : Choose VRD = VRS = VDS = VDD/3

RoT #2 : Choose RS1 = 1/gm

RoT #3 : Choose |Avo| slightly greater than† midrange

Expansion note: why these three rules of thumb

RoT #2 makes gmRS1 = 1, so the gain is halved but depends only half as strongly on gm: if gm changes by 10%, gm/(1+gmRS1) changes by about 5%. RoT #3 allows for the load. The notes' |Avo| is the open-circuit gain; with RL connected the midrange gain comes out a little lower, so |Avo| is chosen a little higher than the midrange gain wanted.

Appendix

A. Problem Types in This Notetaker and How to Solve Them

A1. Designing a common-source amplifier with RS fully bypassed

Recognize it

You are given an open-circuit gain |Avo|, a minimum input resistance RIN, the signal size vg,pk, the supply VDD and the device (Vt, Kn). You must choose RD, RS, RG1 and RG2. A capacitor across RS removes it from the signal path.

Method

  1. Split the supply: VRD=VDS=VRS=VDD/3 (type A2).
  2. Current from the gain: ID=2Kn(VDD/3|Avo|)2.
  3. Resistors: RD=RS=VDD/3ID.
  4. VOV=2ID/Kn, VGS=VOV+Vt, VG=VGS+VS with VS=VDD/3.
  5. Gate divider for VG and RIN (type A3).
  6. Check the output swing (type A4) and linearity (type A5).
  7. Simulate and compare (type A7).

Checks

  • VDS=VDD/3≥VOV (here 5 V against 0.4 V).
  • ID is in the range the device and supply can handle; RD and RS are reasonable.

Variants

  • A different gain changes only ID (it goes as 1/|Avo|2) and everything after it.
  • A standard resistor value slightly off the computed one shifts the gain and bias a few percent; re-evaluate with the built value.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing mA/V² with A/V²: use one system of units throughout.
  • Using VDD instead of VDD/3 in the current formula.
  • Forgetting that VG=VGS+VS, not just VGS.

Worked in these notes: Ex. 1, pp. 18-3 to 18-5; derivation on pp. 18-2 and 18-3.

A2. Splitting the supply between RD, the transistor and RS

Recognize it

"How do we choose the biasing voltages?" The supply is divided among three series elements, and the split sets swing, gain and bias stability.

Method

  1. Write VDD=VRD+VDS+VRS (KVL around the drain-source path).
  2. Give each a share. The rule used here is equal thirds, VDD/3.
  3. Verify VDS≥VOV for saturation.

Variants

  • Need more swing: raise VDS and lower the other two.
  • Need stronger feedback (less sensitivity to Vt and Kn): raise VRS.

Common mistakes

  • Taking VDS below VOV, which puts the device in triode.
  • Forgetting that VRS is also VS, the source voltage above ground.

Worked in these notes: pp. 18-1, 18-2, Ex. 1 Step 1, Ex. 2 Step 1.

A3. Designing the gate divider for VG and RIN

Recognize it

The gate voltage and a minimum input resistance are known; choose RG1 (to VDD) and RG2 (to ground).

Method

  1. RIN=RG1∥RG2=RG1VG/VDD, so RG1≥(VDD/VG)RIN.
  2. RG2=RG1VDD/VG−1.
  3. Choose convenient standard values and recompute VG and RIN.

Checks and mistakes

  • The built RG1∥RG2 must still meet the requirement (Ex. 1: 573.4 kΩ).
  • No gate current flows, so the divider is unloaded; do not include the transistor.
  • Swapping RG1 and RG2 inverts the ratio VG/VDD.

Worked in these notes: Ex. 1 Step 6, pp. 18-4 and 18-5; Ex. 2 Step 7, p. 18-10.

A4. Checking the output swing: VD,max and VD,min

Recognize it

"Check VD,max and VD,min": confirm that the drain voltage, moving by the signal, never leaves saturation.

Method

  1. DC drain level VD=VDD−VRD.
  2. Swing: ±|Avo|vg,pk.
  3. VD,max=VD+|Avo|vg,pk must be below VDD; VD,min=VD−|Avo|vg,pk must stay above VG−Vt.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing VD,min with VS rather than VG−Vt (the saturation limit is on the drain-gate voltage).
  • Using peak-to-peak instead of peak amplitude.

Worked in these notes: Ex. 1 Step 7, p. 18-5; Ex. 2 Step 8, p. 18-10.

A5. Checking small-signal linearity

Recognize it

"Check linearity": make sure the signal at the gate-source junction is small enough for the small-signal gain to apply.

Method

  1. Fully bypassed: vgs,pk=vg,pk; test vgs≪2VOV.
  2. Partial bypass: vgs,pk=vg,pk1+gmRS1; test vgs,pk≤0.2VOV.

Variants

  • A larger input fails the test: raise VOV (more bias current) or add unbypassed RS1.

Common mistakes

  • Using the whole input signal in the partial-bypass test; RS1 takes part of it.
  • Mixing the two criteria (≪2VOV and ≤0.2VOV are different tests in the notes).

Worked in these notes: Ex. 1 Step 8, p. 18-5; Ex. 2 Step 9, p. 18-10.

A6. Designing a common-source amplifier with partial RS bypass

Recognize it

As A1, but the gain must depend less on the device. Part of the source resistance, RS1, stays unbypassed.

Method

  1. VRD=VRS=VDS=VDD/3 (RoT #1).
  2. ID=2Kn(VDD6|Avo|)2.
  3. RD=RS=VDD/3ID.
  4. VOV=2ID/Kn, gm=KnVOV.
  5. RS1=1/gm (RoT #2), RS2=RS−RS1.
  6. VGS=VOV+Vt, VG=VGS+VS; gate divider as in A3.
  7. Checks A4 and A5; simulate.

Checks

  • |Avo|=gmRD/2 reproduces the target.
  • RS1+RS2=RS so the DC bias is the same as with the full resistor.

Variants

  • Other ratios gmRS1 trade gain for stability; the notes use 1.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the capacitor across all of RS (that is the fully bypassed design).
  • Using 3 instead of 6 in the denominator: the extra 2 comes from the gain halving.

Worked in these notes: Proposal #2, pp. 18-7 and 18-8; Ex. 2, pp. 18-9 to 18-11.

A7. Verifying a design in simulation

Recognize it

"Simulate to verify": compare the hand design with a DC operating-point analysis, a transient run and a parameter sweep.

Method

  1. DC operating point: read ID, VD and VS (node voltages and the transistor current).
  2. Transient: place the two cursors on a crest and a trough of the output; dy is the peak-to-peak output.
  3. Gain: |Av|=dy/(2vg,pk) (peak-to-peak output divided by peak-to-peak input).
  4. Sweep Vt and Kn over the stated values and tabulate ID, VD, VS and the gain.

Common mistakes

  • Using dy/dx as the gain.
  • Dividing by the peak input instead of peak-to-peak.
  • Expecting exact agreement: λ and the load make the simulated gain about 2% below the hand value.

Worked in these notes: pp. 18-6 and 18-11.

A8. Predicting how the gain changes from device to device

Recognize it

"Why the variation in Av?" Compare the gain of two devices with different Kn at the same bias current.

Method

  1. Write |Avo|=2KnIDRD.
  2. Divide the two cases: Avo,2Avo,1=Kn2/Kn1 for equal ID and RD.
  3. With partial bypass, use Avo=−gmRD/(1+gmRS1): the change is roughly halved when gmRS1=1.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the square root (the gain goes as Kn, not Kn).
  • Applying the ratio when ID differs substantially between the devices.

Worked in these notes: p. 18-7 (and the comparison in p. 18-11).
