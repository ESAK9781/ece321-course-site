Ex. 1 — Carrier concentrations in doped silicon

In a boron-doped silicon layer with impurity concentration of 1017cm⁻³, find the hole and electron concentrations at 27°C and 125°C.

Boron is an acceptor, so NA=1017cm⁻³. The material is p-type; holes are the majority carriers. At 27°C, ni≈1010cm⁻³:

p0≈NA=1017cm⁻³,n0=ni2NA=(1010)21017=103cm⁻³.

Expansion note: why the minority concentration comes from mass action

At thermal equilibrium, n0p0=ni2. Once the dopant concentration sets the majority-carrier concentration, the minority concentration follows from this product. The source asks for both temperatures; the higher-temperature case is completed symbolically on page 13-2 because its numerical ni value is not supplied in the packet.

13-2

The value of intrinsic concentration changes with temperature. Silicon's intrinsic concentration rises rapidly as temperature rises. The exponential dependence is summarized by ni∝T3/2e−Eg/(2kT).

At 125°C, use the intrinsic concentration for that temperature, ni(125°C), then as before:

p0≈1017cm⁻³,n0=ni(125°C)21017cm⁻³.

When intrinsic carriers outnumber dopant carriers, ni≫NA,ND, the semiconductor approaches intrinsic behavior: n≈p≈ni.

Q1. What type of semiconductor current results from the presence of an electric field? Drift current.

Expansion note: distinguish drift and diffusion

A field produces drift by exerting force on mobile charge. A carrier-concentration gradient produces diffusion as carriers spread from higher concentration toward lower concentration.

Diode fundamentals

A pn junction joins p-type material (NA) and n-type material (ND). For the sketch, the p side is left and the n side is right.

The depletion-region electric field is associated with the built-in (barrier) voltage:

V0=VTln(NANDni2).

The depletion width depends on doping and applied reverse bias:

W=2εsiq(1NA+1ND)(V0+VR).

The p-type and n-type portions of the depletion region are

xp=WNDNA+ND,xn=WNANA+ND.

Ex. 2 — Carrier concentration across the junction

Sketch carrier concentrations at equilibrium, forward bias, and reverse bias against position across a pn junction. The equilibrium sketch shows majority-carrier concentrations set by doping and minority concentrations set by mass action. Forward bias raises the injected minority concentrations near the junction; reverse bias lowers them near the junction.

Q3. Why is there bound charge in the depletion region? Mobile electrons and holes diffuse across the junction and recombine. This leaves behind the fixed, ionized donor and acceptor atoms.

Q4. Why focus on minority concentration? The majority concentration is approximately pinned by doping; diode current is controlled by minority carriers injected across the junction and their diffusion away from it.

Expansion note: why the depletion region widens under reverse bias

Reverse bias adds to the junction barrier. The field must balance the larger potential across the space-charge region, so more fixed ionized dopants are uncovered and the depletion width increases. Forward bias reduces the barrier and narrows the region.

13-5

Diode current predicted by the Shockley relation:

ID=Aqni2(DpLpND+DnLnNA)(eVD/VT−1).

The first factor sets the diode scale current; the exponential term describes how current responds to diode voltage.

Diode models

Ideal: zero drop when on, open circuit when off. Constant-voltage drop (CVD): use a fixed forward drop when on. Shockley: use the exponential current-voltage relation.

Ex. 3 — Diode network

If VIN=5sin(2π×1000t)V, what is VO?

With ideal diodes, positive input forward-biases D1 and the forward-oriented member of the anti-parallel pair, so VO≈VIN. Negative input turns D1 off and the load resistor returns VO to zero. In a CVD analysis, subtract the drops of the conducting diodes from the positive half-cycle.

Expansion note: the answer depends on the selected diode model

The source gives the waveform and circuit but does not name a diode model. The ideal-model result is stated first; the CVD interpretation is also noted so the modeling assumption is explicit.

Ex. 4 — Find current and voltage with the CVD model

Label diode voltages and currents. Assume each diode is on or off, analyze the resulting circuit, and check that each assumed state agrees with the computed voltage/current polarity.

First assume D1 is off and D2 is on. The two resistors and D2 form one path from +3V to −3V:

iD2=3−(−3)−0.71kΩ+3kΩ=1.325mA.

The node voltage is V=3−(1.325mA)(1kΩ)=1.675V. Since this exceeds the 0.7V turn-on level, D1 would conduct; the off assumption is inconsistent.

Expansion note: reject a diode state when the computed voltage contradicts it

With D1 assumed off, its anode-to-cathode voltage must not forward-bias it. The computed node at 1.675V is above ground by more than the CVD threshold, so D1 cannot remain off.

13-7

Now assume D1 and D2 are both on. The node above D1 is 0.7V and the node below D2 is 0V, so

iD2=0−(−3)3kΩ=1mA.

The 1kΩ current is (3−0.7)/1kΩ=2.3mA. By KCL, iD1=2.3−1=1.3mA. Both currents are positive, so both on assumptions are consistent.

Expansion note: check the diode assumptions with current direction

For an assumed-on diode, the CVD model fixes its forward voltage. A positive current in the chosen anode-to-cathode direction validates the assumption. A negative current would require revisiting the state.

Zener diode

In reverse breakdown, a Zener diode supports approximately its rated voltage VZ over a range of current. The small slope of its breakdown curve is represented by incremental resistance.

MOSFETs — structure and symbols

The gate voltage controls channel formation. For an NMOS, a positive VGS above threshold induces an n-type channel between source and drain.

At small VDS, the triode-region current is

iD=kn[(VGS−Vt)VDS−12VDS2].

Close to zero VDS, iD≈kn(VGS−Vt)VDS.

13-12

At the largest VDS before channel pinch-off, the drain-end channel voltage reaches threshold: VGD=Vt, equivalently VDS=VGS−Vt=VOV. In saturation (ignoring channel-length modulation):

iD=12kn(VGS−Vt)2=12knVOV2.

Most problems assume λ=0 unless stated otherwise.

Ex. 7 — Channel-length modulation

An NMOS has k′n=400μA/V², L=0.5μm, W=5μm. Given V′A=5V/μm of channel length, find VA, λ, and iD for VOV=0.25V and VDS=1V.

VA=V′AL=(5V/μm)(0.5μm)=2.5V,λ=1/VA=0.4V⁻¹.

Here kn=k′n(W/L)=400(5/0.5)=4000μA/V². Since VDS=1V>VOV, use saturation with channel-length modulation:

iD=12(4000μA/V²)(0.25V)2(1+0.4V⁻¹·1V)=0.175mA.

Expansion note: distinguish process and device transconductance parameters

The given k′n is normalized per geometry. Multiply by W/L to obtain the device parameter kn. The source gives both channel length and width, so this geometry factor is required.

Appendix

Part A — Problem types

1. Majority and minority carrier concentration

Identify donor or acceptor doping and the majority carrier. Use charge neutrality and mass action, np=ni2, to find the minority concentration. At elevated temperature, use the temperature-specific intrinsic concentration before applying mass action.

2. pn-junction field, voltage, and depletion width

Identify NA, ND, temperature, and bias polarity. Find built-in voltage, add reverse bias to the barrier term, calculate total depletion width, and partition it using charge neutrality.

3. Sketch junction carrier profiles

Mark the depletion region and majority concentrations on each side. At equilibrium, minority concentrations obey mass action. Forward bias raises injected minority concentrations; reverse bias lowers the edge concentrations.

4. Piecewise diode network analysis

Choose ideal or CVD model. Try an ON/OFF state, replace on diodes by their model and off diodes by open circuits, solve with KCL/KVL, then check forward voltage and current consistency.

5. Zener regulation and power limit

Use the linear model around the stated operating point to relate Zener voltage and current. Combine it with the series-resistor relation and the power constraint PZ=VZIZ. Without the rated power, a maximum source voltage is indeterminate.

6. Diode small-signal line regulation

Find the DC current first, replace each forward-biased diode with rd=nVT/ID, and use the incremental circuit to find the output change. Add a load as a parallel small-signal path.

7. MOSFET operating-region current

Compare VDS with VOV=VGS−Vt. Use the triode expression below the boundary and the saturation expression above it. Include (1+λVDS) only when channel-length modulation is included.

Part B — Topics

Semiconductor carrier physics

Doping sets majority-carrier concentration; mass action links majority and minority carriers. Drift is field-driven, diffusion is gradient-driven, and mobility and diffusivity obey the Einstein relation.

pn-junction electrostatics

Diffusion and recombination leave fixed ionized dopants in the depletion region. Built-in voltage and depletion width depend on doping, temperature, and applied bias.

Diode circuit models

Ideal, constant-voltage-drop, and Shockley models trade simplicity for accuracy. A diode-state assumption is valid only when the resulting voltage and current satisfy that state.

Zener and incremental diode behavior

The Zener breakdown region can regulate voltage while the diode stays within current and power limits. Around a DC operating point, the diode is approximated by incremental resistance.

MOSFET channel formation and regions

Gate overdrive forms the channel; the drain voltage determines whether the device is in triode or saturation. Channel-length modulation gives finite output conductance in saturation.
