Lesson 35: SR Latches, Cross-Coupled Inverters and Sizing the Write Transistors

35-1

SRAM Operation

(*) Let's recall where the 6T storage cells lie

and that the storage cell is just a latch† with access† transistors connecting to the bit† & bit-bar† busses

Expansion note: where the cells sit in the memory

The array is a grid of 6T cells. Each row shares a word line W from the row decoder; each column shares a pair of bit lines B and B‾ that run down to that column's sense amplifier and pre-charge circuit, and then to the column decoder. Selecting a word line connects one whole row of cells to their bit lines at once.

35-2

(*) We need a sense†-amplifier and pre-charge† circuit for each B† & B‾† pairs so one pair per column†

Expansion note: why one sense amplifier and pre-charge per column

Only one row is active at a time, but every column carries its own data on its own bit-line pair, so each column needs its own circuit to restore the bit lines to VDD/2 and to amplify the small difference that appears on them. The column decoder then chooses which columns' outputs leave the chip.

35-3

General Observations:

  1. 1.
    Bus capacitances CB† & CB‾† are much larger† than cell† capacitances CQ† & CQ‾†
    Expansion note: why the bit-line capacitance is so large

    A bit line runs the whole height of the array and touches the access transistor of every cell in its column, so its wire and junction capacitance adds up (the text quotes values over 100 fF). A cell's own node capacitance CQ is only a few femtofarads. This ratio is why a cell cannot swing a bit line by itself and why a sense amplifier is needed.

  2. 2.
    φ_S† controls when P_S3† & N_S3† are on† and thus when the sense† amplifier is active†
    → Takes small† differences between V_B† & V_B‾† and if
    VB > VB‾ drives VB= VDD & VB‾= 0
    VB < VB‾ drives VB= 0 & VB‾= VDD
  3. 3.
    φ_P† controls when N_P1† N_P2† N_P3† are on†. These charge B† & B‾† to VDD/2†. NP1 has current flow if VB ≠† VB‾ and works to equalize† them

(*) We need to understand the READ† and WRITE† processes and how the circuit operation imposes sizing† requirements on the transistors†

35-4

READ Operation: Assume Q= 1† Q‾= 0† → B & B‾ will serve as data/sense† lines

  1. STEP 1:
    Set φ_P† =V_DD† so NP1, NP2, NP3 turn on† causing VB & VB‾ → VDD/2
  2. STEP 2:
    Turn pre-charge off (φP=0)
  3. STEP 3:
    Set W=V_DD† → A_1 & A_2 turn on†
    Because CB ≫ CQ, CB‾ ≫ CQ‾:
    VQ barely changes† while VB rises slightly†; VQ‾ barely changes† while VB‾ falls slightly†
  4. STEP 4:
    Set φ_S† =V_DD† Sense Amplifier turns on†
    Feedback: VB → VDD   VB‾ → 0
  5. STEP 5:
    Turn on† column decoder†
Expansion note: why the bit lines move only slightly when the word line rises

After pre-charge both bit lines float at VDD/2. When W rises, the stored node Q (at VDD) is connected to B and the node Q‾ (at 0 V) to B‾. Because the bit lines hold far more charge capacity than the cell nodes, charge sharing moves VB up and VB‾ down by only a few tens of millivolts. That small difference ΔV is what the sense amplifier in step 4 detects and amplifies to full logic levels with its positive feedback.

Expansion note: reading the bit-line waveforms

Both curves start at VDD/2 after pre-charge. At the word-line edge they separate by a small ΔV(1) or ΔV(0) depending on the stored bit. When the sense amplifier is activated it amplifies the difference exponentially, so the upper curve (read 1) goes to V(1)=VDD and the lower curve (read 0) goes to V(0)=0. The B‾ line makes the complementary waveform.

Expansion note: why checking at VDD is enough

A higher bit-line voltage pushes more current into the cell through the access transistor, so it is the harder case for keeping VQ‾ low. If the design holds at VDD it holds with margin at the real pre-charge level VDD/2, which is why the simpler VDD assumption is used.

Expansion note: why A1 is in saturation and N1 in triode

The access transistor A1 has its gate and drain both at VDD and its source at VQ‾=Vtn. Its VGS=VDS=VDD−Vtn always exceeds the overdrive VGS−Vtn=VDD−2Vtn, so it is saturated. The pull-down N1 has its gate at Q=VDD and only Vtn across it, much less than its overdrive VDD−Vtn, so it is a triode resistor to ground.

A. Problem Types in This Notetaker and How to Solve Them

A1. Sequencing an SRAM read

Recognize it

"Describe the steps of a READ" or "what are VB and VB‾ after step k?" (pp. 35-4, 35-5).

Method

  1. Pre-charge: φP=VDD, so VB=VB‾=VDD/2; then φP=0.
  2. Raise W to VDD: access transistors connect the cell; B drifts toward the side holding the 1.
  3. Raise φS: the sense amplifier regenerates the small difference to VDD and 0.
  4. Enable the column decoder to pass the result out.

Checks

  • The difference ΔV is small (tens of millivolts) before step 3 and a full swing after.

Common mistakes

  • Raising φS before ΔV has developed.
  • Leaving pre-charge on while the word line is up.

Worked in these notes: pp. 35-4, 35-5.

A2. Maximum access-transistor size for a non-destructive read

Recognize it

Given VDD and Vtn, find the largest Wa/Wn (pp. 35-6 to 35-9).

Method

  1. Pre-charge both bit lines to VDD (the worst case) and require VQ‾≤Vtn.
  2. A1: VGS=VDS=VDD−Vtn, saturated: IA1=12Kn′(W/L)a(VDD−2Vtn)2.
  3. N1: VGS=VDD, VDS=Vtn, triode: IN1=Kn′(W/L)n[(VDD−Vtn)Vtn−12Vtn2].
  4. Require IA1≤IN1 and solve: WaWn≤1[1−Vtn/(VDD−Vtn)]2−1.

Checks

  • VDD=1.0 V, Vtn=0.35 V gives 3.69; with (W/L)n=2, (W/L)a≤7.4.

Common mistakes

  • Using the triode equation for A1 (it is saturated).
  • Swapping the inequality: the pull-down must be stronger than the access transistor.

Worked in these notes: pp. 35-6 to 35-9.
