Lesson 4: Ideal Diode Analysis and Applications

4-1

Recap

For ND>NA, how does the charge density change with applied voltage?

Expansion note: read the concentration sketches by region

The sketch's horizontal axis crosses p-type material, the depletion region, and n-type material. With ND>NA, the n-side majority-carrier density is higher than the p-side majority-carrier density far from the junction. Applied bias changes the minority-carrier boundary concentrations most strongly: forward bias increases injection, while reverse bias suppresses it. At equilibrium, forward bias, and reverse bias, compare each profile against its far-from-junction majority level.

4-2

Q. Why do we track the carrier concentration?

A. It is what the voltage impacts, and carrier motion is what allows current to flow.

(*) Our result was

I=Aqni2(DpLpND+DnLnNA)(eVD/VT−1).

The first factor is the diode scale current IS; I is current.

Depletion Region Capacitance

(*) We have two effects: depletion-region charge dominates reverse bias, while minority-charge separation dominates forward bias. We will focus on the former.

Q. Why do we care?

Capacitance has effects and will introduce delay in digital circuits.

Expansion note: why minority charge creates forward-bias storage

Forward bias injects minority carriers across the junction. Those carriers must recombine or diffuse away when the voltage changes, so their stored charge makes the diode response depend on time. Reverse bias is dominated by the voltage-dependent depletion region, which behaves as a junction capacitance.

(*) For a pn junction, there is a nonlinear relationship between voltage and charge. At equilibrium it yields

Cj0=Aεsq2V0NANDNA+ND.

Recall the built-in voltage is given by

V0=VTln(NANDni2),VT=kTq≈25mV.

With an applied reverse voltage, this gets modified to

Cj=Cj0(1+VR/V0)m,13≤m≤12,

where m depends on how concentration changes at the junction boundary.

Expansion note: why reverse bias reduces junction capacitance

Reverse voltage widens the depletion region. Since C=εA/W, a larger separation between the fixed charge regions means a smaller junction capacitance. The exponent m reflects how the doping profile changes across the junction.

Ideal Diode Model

Forward bias: VD=0 means the diode is assumed ON; replace it by a short-circuit, so iD>0.

Reverse bias: VD<0 means the diode is assumed OFF; replace it by an open-circuit, so iD=0.

(*) The I-V characteristic becomes the piecewise ideal-diode curve shown above.

Q. How do we generally use this model?

Typically, we assume a state and analyze the circuit with the diode either ON (short) or OFF (open). Then confirm that the assumptions are consistent: ON requires iD>0; OFF requires VD<0.

Expansion note: verify an ideal-diode assumption with the right variable

An ON diode is a short, so solve the circuit and check that current flows in the forward reference direction. An OFF diode is an open, so solve with zero current and check that its voltage is reverse-biased. A contradictory sign means the assumed state must be changed.

Ex. 3   Rectifier

(*) We can find the average from

Vo,avg=12π∫0πVpsin(θ)dθ

=Vp2π(−cos(π)+cos(0))=Vpπ.

Expansion note: average over the full period, including the zero half

The ideal diode is ON for the positive input half-cycle and OFF for the negative half-cycle, so the output is a half-wave rectified sine. It repeats every 2π radians. Its positive half-sine contributes area ∫0πVpsin(θ)dθ=2Vp; the diode blocks the other half-cycle, whose output area is zero. Dividing by the whole period gives Vp/π.

Ex. 4   Battery charger

When does iD flow?

Assume the ideal diode is OFF and replace it by an open.

Vs=24cos(θ)V;iD=Vs−12V100Ω

Conduction requires 24cos(θ)>12, or cos(θ)>12. Over one cycle, −π3<θ<π3 (modulo 2π).

(*) The peak diode current is

Id,peak=24V−12V100Ω=120mA.

Expansion note: a charger diode conducts only above the battery voltage

The battery holds the diode's cathode side at 12 V. The ideal diode turns on only when the source at its anode rises above that level; at equality current is zero. The source peak is 24 V, so the peak resistor drop is 24−12=12 V.

Ex. 5

Find I and V; determine which diodes are ON.

Assume both are ON and replace each by a short. Then

i12k=3V−0V12kΩ=0.25mA,i6k=0V−(−3V)6kΩ=0.50mA.

KCL gives iD1=i12k−i6k=0.25−0.50=−0.25mA. The negative current contradicts the ON assumption for D1, so both-ON is inconsistent.

Expansion note: use KCL to test the shared-node diode current

The 12 kΩ branch supplies 0.25 mA to the node, while the 6 kΩ branch draws 0.50 mA. If D1 were ON with its reference current downward, it would need to supply a negative 0.25 mA. That means D1 cannot be ON under this assumption.

Ex. 6   What if we swap resistors?

(*) Assume both diodes are ON. Then

i6k=3V−0V6kΩ=0.50mA,i12k=0V−(−3V)12kΩ=0.25mA.

KCL: i6k=iD1+i12k, so iD1=0.50−0.25=0.25mA>0. Both ON assumptions are consistent.

Expansion note: swapping the branch resistors changes the KCL balance

With 6 kΩ on the +3 V branch and 12 kΩ on the -3 V branch, the upper branch supplies more current than the lower branch takes. D1 carries the positive difference, so the clamp can remain forward-biased.

Ex. 7

Find I and V for the diode-input network.

(*) Assume D1 is ON and D2, D3 are OFF. The common node is then V=3V and

VD2=2V−3V=−1V,VD3=1V−3V=−2V,

so both OFF assumptions are valid. The load current is iD1=I=3V/1kΩ=3mA>0. The assumptions are consistent.

Expansion note: the highest input wins in a diode OR

The 3 V input forward-biases D1 and raises the output node to 3 V in the ideal model. At that node voltage, the 2 V and 1 V inputs leave their diodes reverse-biased by 1 V and 2 V. The resistor current flows from the highest input through D1 and the 1 kΩ load.

Appendix

Part A   Problem types

Check how applied bias changes junction carrier concentration

Identify equilibrium and the p/n sides. Use doping to establish the far-from-junction majority levels. Under forward bias, raise injected minority concentrations at the depletion boundaries; under reverse bias, lower them. Keep majority levels approximately fixed away from the junction.

Find built-in voltage and depletion capacitance

Use V0=VTln(NAND/ni2), then Cj0=Aεsq(NAND)/(2V0(NA+ND)). Under reverse bias, apply Cj=Cj0/(1+VR/V0)m with the junction-profile exponent given for the device.

Analyze an ideal-diode circuit by state assumptions

Assume each diode ON (replace with a short) or OFF (replace with an open), solve using KVL/KCL, then check consistency. ON must have nonnegative forward current; OFF must have nonpositive anode-to-cathode voltage. If any state fails, try another combination.

Analyze a half-wave rectifier and average output

For an ideal series diode and resistive load, the diode conducts during the positive input half-cycle and blocks the negative half-cycle. Thus vo=Vpsinθ for 0<θ<π and zero for π<θ<2π, giving Vo,avg=Vp/π.

Determine a battery-charger conduction interval

Compare the source voltage with the battery voltage. Conduction occurs only where vs>Vbattery; use that inequality to determine the phase interval. At the peak, Ipeak=(Vs,peak−Vbattery)/R.

Solve a multi-diode clamp or diode-OR network

State each diode's ON/OFF assumption, replace it with the corresponding ideal element, solve node voltages and branch currents, and verify each diode. For a wired diode OR feeding a grounded load, the largest source ordinarily turns on its diode and sets the output.

Part B   Topics touched in the notes

Majority and minority carriers

Doping establishes majority and minority carrier concentrations. Applied voltage changes the carrier injection at a pn junction and therefore the diode current.

Depletion-region charge and capacitance

Fixed ionized dopants in the depletion region store charge. Its width changes with reverse bias, producing junction capacitance; injected minority charge contributes storage under forward bias.

Ideal-diode model and consistency check

An ideal ON diode is a short with forward current; an ideal OFF diode is an open with reverse voltage. The model is piecewise and must be verified against its assumed state.

Rectifier, limiter, clamper and Zener protection

Diodes steer current to rectify AC, clip or shift waveform levels, and clamp input voltage. A Zener protection network uses reverse breakdown to limit a node; the source figure labels a 5.2 V maximum at A0.
