(*) Recall, the charge carrier concentrations in equilibrium, where the generation rate = recombination rate, result in n=p=ni, so that np=ni2.

Expansion note: apply the equilibrium carrier relation carefully

The mass-action law is np=ni2 at thermal equilibrium. Equal electron and hole concentrations, n=p=ni, are the intrinsic case; doping changes the individual concentrations while their product remains ni2 under equilibrium conditions.

DEF. Intrinsic: pure semiconductor, where n=p=ni.

Expansion note: why a donor produces n-type silicon

A group V atom has five valence electrons in a lattice where four are used in covalent bonds. Its extra electron can become mobile, increasing the electron concentration. When donors dominate the intrinsic carriers, charge neutrality gives nn≈ND, while the mass-action law sets the minority-hole concentration to pn=ni2/nn.

2-3

Increase Group III (e.g., boron) → Group (3) → p-type Si.

→ has space for extra electron

→ called an acceptor

so NA concentration

→ The idea is to make NA≫ni, so pp≈NA, which is independent of temperature.

→ So for p-type Si, np=ni2, and np=ni2pp≈ni2NA.

Expansion note: how an acceptor leaves a mobile hole

A group III atom supplies only three valence electrons to the four-bond lattice. It can accept a neighboring bond electron; the missing electron is represented as a hole that can move as nearby electrons fill and vacate bonds. When acceptors dominate intrinsic carriers, pp≈NA and np=ni2/pp.

DEF. Drift current: current due to presence of an electric field.

Expansion note: track conventional current separately from electron motion

For a positive field in the chosen direction, holes drift with the field and electrons drift against it. Current direction also depends on charge sign: multiplying the negative electron charge by its negative drift velocity makes the electron contribution to conventional current point along E.

To turn this into current density, we divide by A.

So Js,p=μpEpq because we track holes.

Expansion note: derive the drift-current density from carrier flow

In time Δt, carriers with drift speed v sweep a volume vAΔt through the cross-section. Multiplying by carrier concentration and charge and dividing by Δt gives current; dividing by area gives J. For electrons, both charge and drift velocity carry a negative sign, so their product is positive in the field direction. The sum is Js=q(pμp+nμn)E.

The pn-Junction

1. Holes are the majority carrier in the p-type material.

2. Electrons are the majority carrier in the n-type material.

(*) Under open-circuit conditions (and not showing the net bound charge)

The pn-junction experiences no external current.

3. Holes diffuse from p to n-region then recombine.

4. Electrons diffuse from n to p-region then recombine.

5. This creates fixed charge and an electric field.

Expansion note: how diffusion leaves a depletion region behind

Holes diffuse from the p side into the n side, and electrons diffuse from n into p. When these carriers recombine near the boundary, the dopant ions remain fixed: negatively charged acceptors on the p side and positively charged donors on the n side. This space charge is the depletion region and establishes an electric field that opposes further majority-carrier diffusion.

Expansion note: why equilibrium has zero net terminal current

The concentration gradients drive diffusion, while the built-in electric field drives drift in the opposite direction. At thermal equilibrium, the two current contributions have equal magnitudes, so their sum at the terminals is zero even though microscopic carrier motion continues.

Appendix

Part A — Problem types

Determine majority and minority carrier concentrations from doping

Identify whether donors or acceptors dominate. For strongly n-type material, use n≈ND and p=ni2/n. For strongly p-type material, use p≈NA and n=ni2/p. Check that the minority concentration is much smaller and that np=ni2.

Find drift velocity, conductivity and resistance of a semiconductor bar

Set the field direction and use vp=μpE and vn=−μnE. Compute σ=q(pμp+nμn) and ρ=1/σ. For a uniform bar of length l and cross-sectional area A, use R=ρl/A. Keep carrier concentration in the units consistent with mobility and resistivity.

Find a diffusion-current density from a carrier profile

Differentiate the concentration profile with respect to position, then apply JD,p=−qDp(dp/dx) for holes or JD,n=qDn(dn/dx) for electrons. Evaluate the derivative at the requested x and preserve the sign. A decreasing hole concentration produces positive hole current in the +x direction.

Explain equilibrium charge and current in a pn junction

List the majority carriers on each side, identify the directions in which holes and electrons initially diffuse, and state that recombination leaves fixed acceptor and donor ions. The separated charge creates the depletion-region field and built-in potential. At equilibrium, drift and diffusion currents balance, leaving zero net external current.

Part B — Topics

Intrinsic semiconductor equilibrium

Thermal generation and recombination balance at equilibrium. For intrinsic material n=p=ni; the equilibrium mass-action relation is np=ni2.

Doping and carrier types

Group V donor impurities contribute electrons and create n-type material. Group III acceptors create holes and p-type material. Under strong doping, the dopant density approximates the majority-carrier concentration; the mass-action law gives the minority population.

Drift and mobility

An electric field causes average carrier drift. Hole velocity follows the field; electron velocity opposes it. Conventional current from both carrier types points along the field because electron charge and electron drift velocity are both negative relative to that direction.

Diffusion and carrier gradients

Random thermal motion produces a net flow from high concentration toward low concentration when concentration varies with position. Diffusion-current equations include a sign difference between hole and electron contributions because their charges differ.

Conductivity and resistivity

Both carrier types contribute: σ=q(pμp+nμn). Resistivity is ρ=1/σ; a uniform bar has R=ρl/A. Doping often changes resistivity by orders of magnitude by increasing the majority-carrier density.

pn-junction formation and equilibrium

Initial diffusion across the p-n boundary leaves fixed ionized dopants. Their separated charge creates the depletion-region electric field and built-in potential. The equilibrium drift current balances the diffusion current.
