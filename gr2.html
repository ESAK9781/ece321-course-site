15MOSFET Amplifier Models
Instructor objectives (6)
  • Understand the 3 parameters of an amplifier behavioral model: Ri, Ro, and Avo
  • Be able to calculate the voltage gain of a multi-stage amplifier using the Ri, Ro, and Avo of each stage
  • Understand how to experimentally determine R, Ro, and Avo using a function generator, potentiometers, and oscope
  • Understand what is meant by signal swing and linearity; understand the terms headroom and legroom
  • Understand how the Voltage Transfer Curve is derived for a Common Source amplifier and how to find pts A and B in the saturation region
  • Understand why we wish to operate between A and B and why the choice of operating point is important

16MOSFET Small-Signal T-Model
Instructor objectives (7)
  • Understand how to find the small-signal voltage gain from the VTC and what the minus sign conveys about input to output
  • Understand how to calculate the voltage gain at pt B of the VTC and that all points between B and A must have a smaller gain magnitude
  • Be able to calculate the maximum allowable input voltage amplitude to keep a CS amplifier in Saturation (no appreciable distortion)
  • Understand the derivation of the small-signal model and what the role of gm is in that model; be able to apply the test on vgs to determine if we are reasonably linear
  • Understand how to replace a transistor by the hybrid pi or T-small signal model and then place the amplifier circuit in the circuit (what is replaced by short circuits etc)
  • Understand what is ro and where it goes in the small-signal model
  • Be able to use the small-signal model to compute the loaded or no-load voltage gain

17Amplifier Configurations
Instructor objectives (5)
  • Understand the role of the coupling capacitors in an amplifier and how to determine their polarity
  • Be able to design a CS (Common Source)-amplifier with no RS resistor
  • Understand why the CS-amplifier with no RS resistor has its operating point vary significantly as parameters Vt and Kn change
  • Be able to analyze a CS-amplifier with no RS for input resistance and output resistance (understand the circuit conditions for each)
  • Understand how source degeneration creates negative feedback that stabilizes the operating point but that it significantly reduces the gain

18Amplifier Design Examples
Instructor objectives (6)
  • Understand why bypassing RS with a large capacitor recovers the amplifier gain while preserving the operating point stability
  • Understand why biasing the drain at 2/3VDD and the source at 1/3VDD centers the drain output variations between cutoff and triode
  • Be able to design a CS-amplifier with RS fully bypassed including checking the output voltage swing and linearity
  • Understand why this amplifier still has variation in gain with Kn
  • Understand what is gained by only partially bypassing RS and how this impacts the design process; know what are acceptable choices for RS1, the part of RS that is not bypassed by a capacitor
  • How does leaving part of RS not bypassed affect the gain and the value of vgs

19Amplifier Design Example
Instructor objectives (4)
  • Understand why a CS-amplifier is not acceptable to drive a low-resistance load with a wide voltage swing
  • Understand how to analyze the small-signal model of a CD (Common Drain) amplifier to find Ri, Ro, and Avo
  • Be able to design RS, RG1, and RG2 for a CD-amplifier with specifications on the load resistance, voltage swing, and input resistance; understand why we impose a minimum permissible drain current and when this current happens relative to the output voltage
  • Understand what is Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) and why we might want to minimize it in an amplifier

25A Day with Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJTs)
Instructor objectives (11)
  • Understand the semiconductor regions of an NPN Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) and how and why those regions are doped as they are
  • Understand that there are 2 diodes in the BJT structure and that for active mode, one must be forward biased and the other reverse biased
  • Understand that BJT current is generated by diffusion and that the reverse biased junction then helps collect that current
  • Understand that β is the current gain parameter of the BJT that relates IB and IC, and that this β can vary significantly with IC and temperature
  • Understand how to use the large-signal model of the BJT to calculate IC and VCE
  • Understand how the BJT small-signal model differs from that of the FET
  • Understand why a BJT is bipolar and why the FET is unipolar
  • Understand that BJT uses diffusion to produce current while the FET uses drift
  • Understand that the BJT requires base current to flow to get collector current while a FET requires no gate current to get drain current
  • Understand what a temperature coefficient is and why it being negative for a BJT can be problematic
  • Understand that the Early Effect happens in both FETs and BJTs and what it is
